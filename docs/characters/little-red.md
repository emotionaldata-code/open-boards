## Basket evidence and timing

The publisher’s [set rulebook, page 16](https://restorationgames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/UM-Little_Red_Riding_HoodvBeowulf-Rules-two-page.pdf) explicitly says that both Little Red’s Basket and What’s That in My Basket? show a **wild** symbol, representing **any one item, not all three**. The initial basket is a non-card marker at the bottom of the discard pile. It is represented by declarative `discardBase` metadata, so it never enters the deck, hand, discard-card counts, recovery options or exhaustion.

The source JSON uses emoji substitutions: **🐺 = pelt, 🌹 = wolfsbane, ⚔️ = knife, 🌟 = wild**. These mappings were checked against the source’s printed [Never Leave the Path](https://static.unmatched.cards/images/umdb/decks/little-red/never-leave-the-path.png) and [A Grimm Tale](https://static.unmatched.cards/images/umdb/decks/little-red/a-grimm-tale.png) images. In particular, A Grimm Tale’s four-health clause requires **pelt**. Some secondary HTML catalogues attach reversed item labels; the printed imagery and UmDb symbols are the import evidence.

Basket conditions read the current top _settled_ discard when each effect resolves. Combat commitments and an unfinished scheme are excluded while resolving. BOOST and forced discards change the basket immediately; after all effects, the exact played card settles on top of intervening discards. Cards returned to hand are not reinserted. A wild Never Leave the Path offers exactly one of its three effects. The starting basket never requires an advance selection. Each time a one-symbol clause matters, its owner privately chooses whether the wild matches or represents a different item. Deliberately missing is legal, including taking only 2 healing from A Grimm Tale or declining Big Eyes’ value ignoring. This follows the wild clarification in the [compiled designer rulings](https://unstat.ru/en/characters/little-red). A legal Big Ears defense declaration itself chooses knife; cancellation cannot undo that declaration.
