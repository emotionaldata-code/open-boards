# Arena interface

The match is a full-viewport game stage: an asymmetric map of stone ruins, gardens and a canal, four distinct hero miniatures plus numbered ally figures, compact player health/movement/card controls, and a private card dock. Desktop puts the hand below the board; short landscape screens put it alongside. Portrait phones keep their connection and show rotation guidance. Fullscreen remains an explicit browser action. Rules are available only on sign-in.

No combat-history or match-summary view is shown. Confirmed card plays, combat, abilities, statuses, healing and non-combat damage open an illustrated review that stays until acknowledged. Movement and other routine events use brief arena notices. The final result stays in the existing turn prompt with a return-to-lobby control. Player controls still open public character information and discards in a native, focus-managed dialog.

## Boundaries

- `board/layout.ts` exposes the shared presentation coordinates. Terrain, figures and HTML hit targets use the same points. Logical edges, portals, spawns and zones live separately in content.
- `board/terrain.ts` draws circular multicolor spaces, portal symbols and routes; `board/scenery.ts` composes static cartography below them; `board/landmarks.ts` owns small drawing primitives. `characters/art.ts` owns shared portrait/miniature paths; `board/figures.ts` renders them in Pixi with player seat colors. Unknown characters fall back to the default Medusa figure. These are original vector drawings, with no artwork dependencies.
- `animation/events.ts` translates viewer-safe confirmed snapshots/outcomes into typed presentation events. Public deck counts produce draw notifications for everyone; private card identities never enter those notifications. Hidden attack cards remain hidden until the server reveals combat.
- `animation/effects.ts` interprets reusable visual operations: path movement, rings, bursts, projectiles/slashes, and floating text. A future ability can compose these operations in a new recipe. A new visual operation needs one renderer implementation. No executable code is accepted from character definitions.
- `animation/queue.ts` orders and deduplicates local playback. `presentation/sequence.ts` coordinates board playback and acknowledgement on that single timeline. New snapshots append instead of cancelling movement. Reconnect clears obsolete effects and unread reviews, then installs the latest board immediately. Authoritative state updates continue independently; local commands wait until pending reviews are acknowledged.
- `presentation/reviews.ts` builds typed readouts from confirmed outcomes and content definitions. `EventDialog.tsx` reuses character portraits and `CardPrint` for readable artwork, full card rules, ability descriptions, and server-confirmed combat damage. `usePresentation.ts` connects the sequence to React; the board only renders effects and never owns acknowledgement. Readouts also work when WebGL is unavailable. New event presentations extend these client recipes without changing networking or rules.
- `game/` owns the HUD, hand, prompts, and public player details. `ui/` owns dialogs, symbols, fullscreen, and rotation guidance. Ability prompts and options are projected per viewer; the client sends a generic choose command. Engine continuations stay private on the server.

The shell uses dynamic viewport units and safe-area insets. Legal destinations and targets have keyboard controls aligned to their circles, capped at 44px. The full map fits its panel with no zoom/pan controls or color legend; the freed space enlarges the board uniformly. The player row and hand dock use wider limits, and landscape phones give the map more of the available width. Player badges and details show current movement through the shared rules calculation, including status modifiers. Hands scroll horizontally; long dialog contents scroll internally. Reduced motion uses stationary effects and the same readable notice durations; it removes travel and dialog motion. Pixi rendering resolution is capped at 2× to keep the map crisp. The canvas follows the displayed board size; static terrain is cached. The board renders on state changes and during active effects, then rests instead of continuously redrawing an idle arena.

Acknowledgement is per viewer and sends no network message. Everyone receives public outcome reviews, while private hooks remain visible only to their recipient. Committed attack cards are never used by presentation recipes; combat readouts show both cards only when the server publishes their reveal. Reviews preserve confirmed outcome order and append new arrivals while reading. Escape and backdrop clicks cannot dismiss them. Their acknowledgement button stays visible while long rules scroll, and reduced motion never auto-dismisses a review.

## Crossroads portals and sound

The board has 33 spaces, five overlapping zones and four diamond-marked portals. Original vector cartography follows the circular spaces and colored zones of [Unmatched](https://iellogames.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Unmatched-CobbleFog_Rulebook_EN_Light.pdf), without copying its board artwork. Any portal can reach another for one movement point. Occupancy and movement budgets still apply. Only movement uses these links; attack adjacency and push/pull distances use physical edges. Portal IDs are optional serializable map content, validated alongside the graph. Existing node IDs and spawns remain compatible. The 1900×780 board has single-entry garden, observatory and dock branches, plus a northern dead end; portals sit outside those branches. The central plaza retains alternate routes. The arena aspect ratio comes directly from shared presentation dimensions. Terraced walls, a roofed west gate, a courtyard fountain, a ruined observatory, bridges, an aqueduct and a timber harbor are original vector artwork.

`animation/travel.ts` animates confirmed walks and portal fades. `animation/playback.ts` schedules visuals and sound together and cancels both. `audio/cues.ts` contains synthesized voice recipes; the header speaker button unlocks audio through `audio/player.ts`. Burn application and its Smolder ability use a crackling fire cue; removing Burn is silent. Portal travel has a rising warp cue. No media downloads or new dependencies are required.

Map review: the board is the largest element, circles carry zone colors and letters, and distinct landmarks make the outer loops recognizable. Desktop and landscape phone screenshots were reviewed separately from the unauthenticated style inspector. The whole map remains visible at every size; movement remains visible on player badges, including small landscape phones. Design 8/10, usability 8/10, creativity 8/10, content 9/10. Small-phone labels remain compact; destination controls retain accessible space labels.

The branch-map update was reviewed through the actual Pixi `Board` component at desktop, tablet, portrait and two landscape phone sizes. Its four content tests check connectivity, duplicate edges, valid coordinates/spawns and the three single-entry branches, including portal links. Scoped lint and the client production build pass. The player-to-fighter migration now updates the protocol, browser and fixtures together; current verification is recorded in verification.md. The temporary preview used for review was removed afterward. No external artwork or new dependencies were added.

## Allied fighters

A compact portrait row switches fighters with one click or keyboard activation. It shows names and ally numbers, with a pressed state on the selected piece. Independent ally health stays visible in each player’s top card, alongside the hero’s health, movement and card count; detailed reach, abilities and statuses remain in player details. During deployment the picker shows unplaced allies; during Maneuver it shows only pieces that have not moved and shows the remaining count and selected movement allowance. Stay applies to the selected piece; Finish Maneuver stays all remaining pieces through normal hooks. Reconnect restores both setup and movement progress. Board targets identify the exact enemy fighter and owner, and defense always filters against the attacked piece. Card tags appear in hand cards, galleries and inspectors.

The Pixi scene draws one miniature per placed fighter, with owner seat colors and numbered ally labels. Actors, movement and damage feedback are keyed by fighter ID; defeated pieces cease occupying spaces. Medusa and Harpies use original code-native vector art. Character exploration includes ally count, HP, reach and passive rules. Player details include the fighters’ health, counters, statuses and abilities. Existing keyboard, rotation, reduced-motion and sound behavior is preserved.
